# LunarWing v2.0.3.0 Codename Ideas

LunarWing v2.0.3.0 is a Rares release: an odd-numbered cycle centered on bug
fixes, security, and polish. The strongest codenames therefore describe careful
reinforcement, durable finishing, secure joins, or restoration rather than a
new frontier. The candidates below follow the Japanese word/name convention
established by `Kawarimi`, `Takamaru`, `Tatara`, `Kiyome`, `Togishi`, and
`Dōkutsu`, without repeating any previous release codename.

## Candidates

### 1. Sashiko (刺し子)

**English context:** Reinforcement stitching; decorative mending.

Sashiko uses many small, deliberate stitches to repair worn cloth and make it
stronger. That is an unusually close match for a release composed of targeted
Engine V2 and reflex-compiler fixes, XMPP hardening reviews, dependency
maintenance, and operational polish, while the restored WeeChat buffers and
Hermes migration path carry the mending metaphor directly.

### 2. Kasugai (鎹)

**English context:** A metal clamp or cramp that draws timbers together and
keeps a structural joint from pulling apart.

Kasugai fits the release's connective work: host-local MCP lifecycle,
NetBird-backed tunnels, XMPP file transfer, and the Kawarimi Hermes adapter all
make separate systems meet reliably. It also conveys hardening—the small,
purpose-built fastener that keeps the larger structure sound.

### 3. Urushi (漆)

**English context:** Japanese lacquer; a layered protective finish valued for
durability and lustre.

Urushi captures both halves of the Rares cadence: protection and polish. The
release applies another durable layer over the v2 foundation through Engine V2
hardening, XMPP review work, safer operations, crate updates, and refinement of
existing interfaces.

### 4. Odoshi (縅)

**English context:** The lacing that binds the individual plates of traditional
Japanese armor into a flexible protective whole.

Odoshi combines a warrior-era image with a precise systems metaphor: the
release strengthens the connections among MCP processes, tunnels, messaging,
migration tooling, and multi-tenant operations. Each component keeps its own
role, but tighter lifecycle and interface work makes the assembled whole more
resilient.

### 5. Kusabi (楔)

**English context:** A wedge or locking key used to tighten a joint and prevent
parts from slipping free.

Kusabi suits a release that closes gaps and tightens boundaries rather than
replacing the structure. MCP lifecycle control, Engine V2 ownership and
reliability work, XMPP hardening, and the mt-admin refactor all act like small
wedges driven into critical joints.

### 6. Tsugite (継手)

**English context:** A traditional timber splice or joining joint.

Tsugite emphasizes continuity across boundaries: Hermes state is joined to a
new LunarWing tenant, host-local MCP servers gain managed lifecycles, and
NetBird replaces proprietary tunnel paths without abandoning the systems they
connect. The name also fits the mt-admin refactor's goal of improving internal
structure while preserving the operator-facing entrypoint.

### 7. Shikkui (漆喰)

**English context:** Traditional lime plaster used as a durable, protective
finish on Japanese buildings and castle walls.

Shikkui is a finishing layer that also hardens what it covers, making it a
natural odd-release metaphor. It fits the combined security reviews, XMPP
file-transfer scrutiny, dependency cleanup, and Engine V2 polish that protect
the existing v2 structure.

### 8. Kintsugi (金継ぎ)

**English context:** “Joining with gold”; repairing broken ceramics with
lacquer and a visible gold-finished seam.

Kintsugi highlights restoration without pretending the original foundation
must be discarded. WeeChat buffer recovery, the Hermes-to-LunarWing migration
adapter, tunnel-provider replacement, and compatibility-focused engine work all
turn repaired joins into intentional parts of the system.

### 9. Tsukuroi (繕い)

**English context:** Mending, repair, or putting something back in good order.

Tsukuroi is the plainest expression of the Rares cadence: repair what is worn,
restore what was lost, and leave the whole cleaner than before. It covers the
release's buffer restoration, migration safety, lifecycle fixes, hardening
reviews, refactors, and crate maintenance without overstating any one feature.

### 10. Ishigaki (石垣)

**English context:** A stone rampart or castle wall built from carefully fitted
stones.

Ishigaki frames v2.0.3.0 as defensive work on an existing stronghold. Its fitted
stones mirror the release's many distinct pieces—MCP, NetBird, XMPP, Engine V2,
mt-admin, Kawarimi, WeeChat, the reflex compiler, and dependencies—strengthened
individually and made more dependable together.

## Top picks

### 1. Sashiko

Best overall: it expresses small, careful fixes that simultaneously restore, reinforce, and refine the existing fabric of LunarWing v2.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  刺し子
## English Context:   Sashiko — reinforcement stitching
```

### 2. Kasugai

Best structural metaphor: it unifies the release's lifecycle, tunnel, file-transfer, migration, and administration work as secure joins.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  鎹
## English Context:   Kasugai — structural clamp
```

### 3. Urushi

Best cadence metaphor: a durable protective finish directly captures both hardening and polish.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  漆
## English Context:   Urushi — Japanese lacquer
```

## Summary

These candidates follow LunarWing's established convention of a single Japanese
word in romaji with native script, drawn from traditional craft or warrior-era
culture and tied directly to the release's real work. For this odd-numbered
Rares cycle, the list favors reinforcement, restoration, secure joins, and
protective finishing; **Sashiko (刺し子)** is the top pick because its pattern of
many small stitches making existing fabric stronger most completely reflects
v2.0.3.0.
