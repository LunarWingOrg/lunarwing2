# LunarWing v2.1.4.0 Codename Ideas

LunarWing v2.1.4.0 is the cycle that repaired, re-audited, and re-armored.
The version sweep moved every LunarWing-owned package from `2.1.3` to `2.1.4`
and took 121 external crates along with it, `ic/vendor/` was regenerated, and
the tracked manifest/lock audit confirmed nothing project-owned was left
behind. The external-crate effort doubled as the security audit (item 3):
every `ic/deny.toml` advisory ignore was re-verified against live crates.io
metadata, the resolved worker-image graph was scanned patched-vs-unpatched, a
full-repo yanked-crate pass retired the single yanked crate in the tree
(chacha20 0.10.1, inherited from upstream Pebble's lock), and the Pebble lock
guard gained multi-version record keying and no-op-override rejection. CI
recovered from pipeline #4679's Cavepony release-metadata drift and
orphan-mission boundary violation.

The feature work carries the same shape — things that were stalled, dropped,
or rejected were recovered rather than abandoned. Engine missions recover
from transient provider failures after partial text instead of aborting the
thread (WI#244); global mission listings no longer drop missions whose
project record is missing, so daily rollovers keep firing (WI#243);
`routine_history` resolves Engine V2 mission names (WI#265); the voice
sidecar bounds abandoned inference admission so a timed-out or cancelled call
cannot pin the model mutex (WI#264); and shared tool execution learned bounded
per-user EMA runtime estimates surfaced as advisory hints on approvals
(WI#191). Five of this cycle's goal items (4, 5, 6, 7, 11) were re-attempts
of previously rejected MRs, each repaired against its review findings until
it merged. The operator surface gained the per-tenant system-prompt editor
with Engine V2 initialization snapshots (WI#220), read-only mt-admin
diagnostics verbs (WI#268), and a pairing allowlist fix so approving a new
sender no longer erases existing grants.

Taken together, this is a release about **completing the armor: inspecting
every seam, repairing what failed, and crowning the harness that protects the
engine**. The candidates therefore favor the armor lineage opened by `Yoroi`
(v2.1.2.0) and `Ō-Yoroi` (v2.1.3.0), appraisal and provenance, repair, and
guarded entry. They follow LunarWing's established Japanese word/name
convention and do not repeat a previous release codename (`Kawarimi`,
`Takamaru`, `Tatara`, `Kiyome`, `Togishi`, `Dōkutsu`, `Sashiko`, `Odoshi`,
`Kagi`, `Kairo`, `Mihari`, `Sekisho`, `Inazuma`, `Ma-ai`, `Zanshin`,
`Yoroi`, `Ō-Yoroi`).

## Candidates

### 1. Kabuto (兜)

**English context:** A great helmet; the crowned piece of Japanese armor.

Kabuto completes the armor line this codename family already opened: `Yoroi`
(v2.1.2.0, the armor), `Ō-Yoroi` (v2.1.3.0, the great armor), and now the
helmet that crowns the set. Armor without a helmet leaves the head exposed,
and the head is exactly what this cycle protected: the engine's mission
execution (WI#244 recovery, WI#243 rollover), the voice sidecar's model
admission (WI#264), and the CI pipeline that gates every merge. The name is
short, unmistakable, and continues a lineage instead of starting a new one.

### 2. Kiwame (極め)

**English context:** A definitive appraisal verdict; the attribution an
expert inscribes after authenticating a blade.

Kiwame names the release's audit spine. The crate security re-audit (item 3)
did not merely scan — it re-verified every accepted advisory against live
crates.io metadata, proved patched-vs-unpatched with a deliberate control
failure, and retired the one yanked crate anywhere in the tree. Kiwame also
fits the five re-attempted MRs: each was examined against its reviewer's
findings and only then judged ready. The word continues the sword-craft
thread `Togishi` (v2.0.1.0, the polisher) began.

### 3. Shūfuku (修復)

**English context:** Repair and restoration; returning a thing to sound
condition.

Shūfuku is the plainest name for the cycle's dominant verb. The integration
pipeline was repaired (item 1), mid-stream provider failures are repaired
without losing the thread (WI#244), missions whose project record went
missing are restored to the global listings that drive their daily rollover
(WI#243), abandoned inference calls release their permits and let the
sidecar recover (WI#264), and the pairing allowlist no longer loses existing
grants when a new sender is approved. Distinct from the v2.0.5.0 candidate
`Kaifuku` (回復, recovery of a condition): shūfuku is the mending of the
thing itself.

### 4. Aratame (改め)

**English context:** Inspection; the official seal certifying a thing has
been examined (aratame-in, the inspection stamps of Edo-period publishers).

Aratame suits a release stitched through with audits and gates: the crate
security audit, the open-work-items audit (item 14), the WI#268
prompt-overlay audit runbook, the execution gate and approval pending state
that gained advisory runtime hints (WI#191), and the boundary ratchet CI
enforces on every MR. The inspection seal was renewed each period — an apt
image for advisory ignores that must be re-justified every release rather
than silently carried forward.

### 5. Mei (銘)

**English context:** The smith's signature inscribed on a blade; the
provenance that authenticates a work.

Mei captures the version sweep. Every LunarWing-owned package now carries
the `2.1.4` mark — daemon, five workspace crates, XMPP bridge, four WASM
channels, and every registry entry — with a tracked-manifest audit proving
no 2.1.3 signature survived. Worker pins are verified the way a blade's
inscription is read: opencode2 v2.0.20's tag SHA was verified live against
the upstream remote, and the Pebble lock's `PEBBLE_LOCK_SHA256` guards its
exact contents. The registry guard now rejects a no-op override, so a false
signature cannot pass for a true one.

### 6. Kintsugi (金継ぎ)

**English context:** "Joining with gold"; repairing broken ceramics with a
visible gold seam rather than hiding the break.

Carried forward from the v2.0.3.0 and v2.0.5.0 shortlists because no cycle
has fit it better: five previously rejected MRs (items 4, 5, 6, 7, 11) were
re-applied and repaired against their review findings, with the fixes —
byte-safe summary truncation, strict legacy matching contracts, keyed name
privacy — recorded visibly in the DONE trails rather than papered over. The
repair is part of the artifact's history.

### 7. Fukigaeshi (吹返し)

**English context:** The turned-back side flanges of a kabuto, bearing the
wearer's crest — the mark of who the armor belongs to.

Fukigaeshi speaks to the identity work: the pairing allowlist fix that
preserves each tenant's approved senders, the per-tenant system-prompt
editor (WI#220) that lets each tenant inscribe its own instructions, and
the `ORCHESTRATOR_SELF_MODIFY` default that makes new tenants' ownership
explicit. It also completes the kabuto itself, pairing naturally with
candidate 1.

### 8. Monban (門番)

**English context:** A gatekeeper; the one who stands at the door and
decides who may enter.

Monban names the admission work: the voice sidecar's bounded permits
(WI#264), the execution gate and approval flows that gained runtime
estimates (WI#191), pairing approvals, sandbox boundaries, and the boundary
ratchet CI posts over every merge. Where `Sekisho` (v2.0.8.0) was the
checkpoint that inspected travelers, monban is the keeper who holds the
door through recovery — permits are released, retried, and never leaked.

### 9. Suzuri (硯)

**English context:** An inkstone; the surface on which ink is ground and
writing is prepared.

Suzuri fits the operator-writing surface this cycle added: the per-tenant
system-prompt editor (WI#220) with draft preservation, workspace-write
validation, and scope isolation, feeding the Engine V2 initialization
snapshot. The inkstone is where words are prepared before they are spoken —
precisely where a system prompt sits relative to every conversation.

### 10. Mekiki (目利き)

**English context:** The practiced eye of a connoisseur; discernment built
from examining many real works.

Mekiki describes how the audit was actually done: not a mechanical scan but
judgment — advisory ignores re-verified against live upstream metadata, a
control run proving the scanner catches a mutated checksum, yanked versions
hunted across every lockfile root. Where v2.0.5.0's `Kantei` named the act
of appraisal, mekiki names the eye that performs it.

## Top Picks

### 1. Kabuto

Best overall. It completes the `Yoroi` → `Ō-Yoroi` armor lineage with the
one piece still missing, and the release genuinely is the helmet: the work
protects the engine's head — mission execution, provider-failure recovery,
rollover correctness, and the CI gate that guards every merge. Short,
distinctive, and instantly explicable in release notes.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  兜
## English Context:   Kabuto — great helmet; the crown that completes the armor
```

### 2. Kiwame

Best audit metaphor. The release's defining rigor is verdicts earned by
examination — re-verified advisories, control-failure proofs, re-attempted
MRs judged against their findings — and kiwame is exactly the word for a
verdict an expert stands behind.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  極め
## English Context:   Kiwame — definitive appraisal; the verdict earned by examination
```

### 3. Shūfuku

Best recovery metaphor. Repair is the cycle's most common verb across
pipeline, engine, voice, pairing, and CI, and the word is plain enough to
translate the release to anyone in one line.

```markdown
## Release Codename:  修復
## English Context:   Shūfuku — repair and restoration to sound condition
```

## Recommendation

Use **Kabuto (兜)** for LunarWing v2.1.4.0. The previous two releases
committed this codename family to the armor of the samurai — `Yoroi`, then
`Ō-Yoroi` — and v2.1.4.0 is the cycle that crowned it: every seam inspected
(the crate and work-item audits), every break mended (pipeline recovery,
mid-stream engine recovery, rollover restoration), and the head finally
guarded. The name rewards the lineage without depending on it; on its own it
still says exactly what this release did for the platform.
