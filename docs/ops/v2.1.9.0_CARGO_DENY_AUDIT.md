# Crate Security Audit — v2.1.9.0

**Date:** 2026-11-07 (pre-release checklist item #2).

**Audited source:** branch `aj/kimi-2-20261107-0000`, commit
`e9815abc575df7559d95013e85a24a5cb6c479ba`.

**Scope:** all **27 tracked non-vendor `Cargo.lock` roots**, including every
`ic/` workspace member and satellite manifest, the root-level workers and
sidecars, both Pebble locks, and the manifest-less Pebble worker-image override.
Lockfiles inside `ic/vendor/` are not independent build roots; their crates are
covered through the resolved graphs and lockfiles of those 27 roots. The audit
used the real crates.io index for the CI-equivalent gates, a fresh RustSec
advisory database, an independent lockfile-text scan, the default-production
and lockfile-only reachability guards, and the pinned Pebble override verifier.

Item #1 had not landed on the audited commit: the daemon still reports 2.1.8
and the item-1 checkbox is open. This report therefore certifies the exact
source above, not a future post-sweep release head. **Re-run the advisory gates
and lockfile scan after item #1 and before release.**

**Previous report:**
[`v2.1.8.0_CARGO_DENY_AUDIT.md`](v2.1.8.0_CARGO_DENY_AUDIT.md).

## Result

- **No new, unaccepted vulnerability was found on any of the 27 roots.**
- **No yanked crate was reported** by the CI-equivalent resolved-graph gates or
  by the independent `cargo-audit` lockfile pass. The main and satellite gates
  emitted zero `index-failure` diagnostics, so their `yanked = "deny"` policy
  was actually enforced rather than skipped.
- The raw lockfile scan found 11 distinct vulnerability IDs. Nine are existing,
  narrowly scoped `ic/deny.toml` acceptances; the other two are the known
  `pyo3 0.28.3` lockfile-only pins, which the reachability guard proves are not
  resolved into either guarded build graph. There are still **13 total advisory
  acceptances**, including informational/unsound entries; none was added,
  removed, or widened.
- One new **informational maintenance notice** is present:
  `RUSTSEC-2026-0444` for `astral-tl 0.8.0`. It is not a vulnerability. The
  current parent can move off this crate; see the follow-up below.
- No dependency, lockfile, vendored source, or advisory policy was changed by
  this audit.

This is not a claim that the accepted residual exposures are harmless. The
opt-in libSQL legacy TLS chain, the tenant RSA signing path, and the vendored
Signal wrapper's `failure` advisory remain documented risks with existing
clearing owners.

graph restrictions and reachability guards are green.

### Lockfile-only IDs (not acceptances)

`RUSTSEC-2026-0176` and `RUSTSEC-2026-0177` match `pyo3 0.28.3` in the `ic` and
XMPP bridge lock text. `pyo3` is behind jiter's optional `python` feature and
is not resolved into either `--workspace --all-features --target all` graph.
`.gitlab/scripts/check-lockfile-only-advisories.sh` proves that absence and
will fail if a future feature change makes either advisory reachable. These two
IDs are not hidden by an ignore.

## New maintenance notice and follow-up

`RUSTSEC-2026-0444` marks every `astral-tl` release unmaintained following its
2026-10-05 deprecation. It has `informational = "unmaintained"` and
`patched = []`; it does **not** describe a memory-safety or other security
vulnerability.

The crate is in the default production graph, not merely lockfile text:

```text
astral-tl v0.8.0
└── html-to-markdown-rs v3.19.0
    └── lunarwing v2.1.8
```

The default `html-to-markdown` feature enables this path. Live crates.io
metadata shows that newest `html-to-markdown-rs 3.21.1` now requires
`astral-html =0.0.4` and no longer depends on `astral-tl`. Item #1's external
crate sweep should therefore take the compatible parent update, regenerate the
affected locks and `ic/vendor/` through the normal tooling, and re-run this
audit. No dependency update was attempted in this item-2 report-only change.

For completeness, a temporary policy copy with `unmaintained = "all"` emitted
10 unignored maintenance diagnostics in the full `ic` graph: the new
`astral-tl`, plus `atomic-polyfill`, `atty`, `bincode`, `fxhash`,
`rustls-pemfile`, `ttf-parser`, `zbus_names`, `zvariant`, and
`zvariant_derive`. That intentional enumeration exits 1; the committed policy
uses `unmaintained = "workspace"` and its gate is green. Accepted `failure` and
`rental` notices additionally appear in the raw `ic` scan. Both Pebble locks
carry only `bincode 1.3.3` and `yaml-rust 0.4.5` unmaintained notices. These
are maintenance statuses, not vulnerability findings.
